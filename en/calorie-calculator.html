Frequently asked questions

FAQ about the calorie calculator

Brief answers to help you understand the tool's limitations and use it more wisely.

What's the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR refers to the energy the body needs at rest, while TDEE adds the effect of movement and daily activity. The calculator provides a guideline estimate, not a laboratory measurement.

Does the calorie calculator result diagnose my health condition?

No. The result is a general estimate to help you understand your daily needs, and it is not used to diagnose a disease, treat a condition, or replace guidance from a dietitian.

When should I recalculate my calorie needs?

You can recalculate when there is a noticeable change in weight, activity, or goal, and compare overall trends in habits and outcomes rather than treating the number as fixed.

How do I use the suggested number safely?

Use it as a starting point and a reference, and focus on balanced meals and appropriate activity. During pregnancy, chronic illness, or when you have a therapeutic goal, consult a specialist before changing your regimen.

Warning: This information is general educational material and does not replace a doctor's assessment or emergency services when needed.
