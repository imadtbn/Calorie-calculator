Educational screening

Asthma control test

Answer the five questions to form an initial picture of symptom control over the past four weeks.

1. During the past four weeks, how often did asthma stop you from doing what you wanted at work, school, or home?
2. How often did you experience shortness of breath?
3. How often did asthma symptoms wake you at night?
4. How often did you use your rescue inhaler or nebulizer?
5. How would you describe your overall asthma control?
ⓘThis result does not diagnose a medical condition or replace a healthcare professional.

How to read the result?

The result is an awareness indicator based on your answers, not a diagnosis or final judgment. If symptoms persist or affect your life, book an appointment with your doctor or an appropriate specialist.

Warning: If you have severe or emergency symptoms, seek urgent care instead of relying on any online questionnaire.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions about the Asthma Control Test

Brief answers to help you understand the tool's limitations and use it more wisely.

What does the Asthma Control Test measure?

It asks about asthma symptoms and their impact over the past four weeks to provide an awareness indicator of the reported level of control.

Does the test result replace the doctor's plan?

No. The result does not diagnose asthma or change medication, and any change in symptoms or frequent use of rescue medication should be discussed with a doctor.

When should I repeat the assessment?

It can be used to monitor changes over time according to your care plan, with accurate answers and without comparing the results to a self-diagnostic standard.

When are symptoms an emergency?

Severe difficulty breathing, inability to speak, or blue lips require calling emergency services immediately instead of waiting for the tool's result.

Warning: This information is general educational material and does not replace a doctor's assessment or emergency services when needed.
