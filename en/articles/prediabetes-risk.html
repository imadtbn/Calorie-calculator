Prediabetes risk test: what does the result mean?

Awareness of risk factors is an early step, but blood tests and a physician's evaluation are the reference for diagnosis.

Last updated: 22 August 2026 · Prepared by: Calorie Calculator team

Totals Prediabetes risk test Points for factors such as age, BMI, family history, blood pressure, physical activity, and some pregnancy-related factors. According to the CDC, a total of 5 points or more indicates higher risk and warrants discussing screening with a healthcare provider.

What do the points measure?

The points do not measure glucose level directly. They are a simplified way to combine factors associated with higher likelihood, so a person with a low score may still need testing based on age, symptoms, or medical history.

What should I do after a high result?

Do not self-diagnose or start treatment based on the result alone. Schedule an appointment and ask about tests such as A1C (glycated hemoglobin) or others as determined by your clinician, and discuss realistic changes to activity, diet, and sleep.

The questionnaire estimates risk; blood tests are what help a clinician assess prediabetes or diabetes.

Can risk factors be reduced?

Regular physical activity, improving diet quality, adequate sleep, and weight management when needed can help support overall health. Choose gradual changes and consult a healthcare professional if you have a chronic condition or take medications.
