Infant Growth

How do we read an infant's length on the WHO chart?

Recumbent length, percentiles, and Z-scores are tools for understanding growth, but they do not replace pediatric follow-up.

Last updated: 22 August 2026 · Prepared by: Calorie Calculator team

Helps Infant Growth Calculator Compares optional recumbent length and weight to the infant's age and sex using WHO standards. The result is a preliminary statistical description and not a diagnosis.

Why use recumbent length?

Before age two, a child's length is usually measured with the child lying on a suitable surface. The head and legs should be held as still as possible, and measurement is preferably done by two people or a trained caregiver to reduce error.

What do percentiles and Z-scores mean?

A percentile describes a measurement's position compared with children of the same age and sex in the reference. A Z-score expresses how far the measurement is from the reference mean in standard deviation units. Neither alone indicates that the infant is healthy or has a problem.

A single measurement is a snapshot; a growth trend across multiple visits gives better context.

When should I see a doctor?

Seek medical assessment for ongoing concerns about feeding, weight loss, slowed length growth, or worrying symptoms. A clinician may review weight and head circumference, perform a clinical exam, and take a dietary history.
