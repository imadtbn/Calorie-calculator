BMI for children: why do we use percentiles?

In children, age and sex are essential to interpreting BMI.

Last updated: 22 August 2026 · Prepared by: Calorie Calculator team

BMI is calculated from weight and height, but its interpretation in a child differs from that in an adult. In BMI calculator for children The result is compared to age and sex using growth chart percentiles, rather than applying a fixed cutoff of 25 or 30 directly.

What does percentile mean?

If the result is near the 75th percentile, that means the BMI is higher than about 75% of reference peers of the same age and sex. A percentile alone does not mean the child is healthy or ill; it helps the doctor track trends and growth.

Child BMI is a screening tool for growth, not a complete assessment of a child's health or diet quality.

What are the general categories?

According to the CDC, below the 5th percentile is underweight; from the 5th to less than the 85th percentile is a healthy weight range; from the 85th to less than the 95th percentile is overweight; and the 95th percentile and above is obesity. These cutoffs should be interpreted within the child's history, examination, and the rate of change in measurements.

What should I do after the result?

Avoid negative comments or extreme diets. If you have concerns about growth, appetite, or weight changes, book an appointment with a pediatrician or a pediatric dietitian.
