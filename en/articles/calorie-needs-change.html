Understanding the Numbers

Why Do Calorie Needs Differ Between People?

There is no single daily number that fits everyone; needs change with body, activity, goals, and daily circumstances.

Last updated: 20 August 2026 · Prepared by: Calorie Calculator Team

Two people can eat the same food but require different amounts of energy. Calorie needs aren't fixed; they're estimates affected by age, height, weight, sex, activity level, and goals.

What is BMR?

BMR (basal metabolic rate) is the approximate energy your body needs at rest to perform basic functions. It doesn't represent everything you burn in a day because it doesn't include walking, work, exercise, or digestion.

What is TDEE?

TDEE (total daily energy expenditure) is the approximate total daily burn after adding activity and movement to basal metabolism. That's why TDEE is usually higher than BMR and changes with your activity or weight.

Use Calories, BMR, and TDEE Calculator Use it as a starting point, and treat the number as an adjustable estimate, not an absolute lab finding.

Five Factors That Change Needs

  1. Weight and height: Body size affects the energy needed to support its functions.
  2. Age: Needs may change across life stages, but health can't be reduced to age alone.
  3. Activity: Daily movement, exercise, and physical work can raise energy expenditure.
  4. Goal: Calorie use differs depending on maintaining weight, changing it, or supporting training.
  5. Routine: Sleep, stress, illness, and shifts in activity can make actual needs fluctuate day to day.
The 'best' number is a starting point to review over time, not a target to reach regardless of your body's signals.

How to Read the Result Without Overreacting?

Use an average over several days instead of judging a single day. Monitor hunger, energy, performance, and weight change over weeks, and consult a professional if you have illness, pregnancy, a medical goal, or a history of eating disorders.
