Educational screening

Mood and depression questionnaire

Over the past two weeks, how often have you been bothered by the following symptoms? If you have thoughts of self-harm, seek urgent help immediately.

1. Little interest or pleasure in doing things?
2. Feeling down, depressed, or hopeless?
3. Trouble falling or staying asleep, or sleeping too much?
4. Feeling tired or having little energy?
5. Poor appetite or overeating?
6. Feeling bad about yourself — or that you are a failure or have let yourself or your family down?
7. Trouble concentrating on reading or watching TV?
8. Moving or speaking so slowly that others notice, or being so fidgety or restless that you are moving more than usual?
9. Thoughts that you would be better off dead or of hurting yourself?
ⓘThis result does not diagnose a medical condition or replace a healthcare professional.

How to read the result?

The result is an awareness indicator based on your answers, not a diagnosis or final judgment. If symptoms persist or affect your life, book an appointment with your doctor or an appropriate specialist.

Warning: If you have severe or emergency symptoms, seek urgent care instead of relying on any online questionnaire.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions about the mood and depression questionnaire

Brief answers to help you understand the tool's limitations and use it more wisely.

What time period does the mood questionnaire cover?

It asks about mood and certain symptoms over the past two weeks to encourage noticing changes and seeking appropriate support.

Does the questionnaire result diagnose depression?

No. It is an awareness screening that does not confirm a diagnosis or prescribe treatment; discuss results with a professional if concerned.

What should I do if my score is high?

Contact a doctor or mental health professional, and share how you’re feeling with someone you trust. Do not stop prescribed medication or start self-treatment based on this result.

What should I do if I have thoughts of self-harm?

Seek emergency help immediately—contact emergency services or your local crisis line and stay close to someone you trust. Do not wait for the questionnaire result.

Warning: This information is general educational material and does not replace a doctor's assessment or emergency services when needed.
