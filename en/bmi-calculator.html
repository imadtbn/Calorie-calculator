What is BMI?

It is an approximate calculation comparing weight to height. It does not distinguish between muscle and fat, so it’s best read alongside other measures.

Under 18.5

Below the common range; discuss with a professional if accompanied by symptoms or unintended weight loss.

18.5 to 24.9

A commonly used adult range, but it does not alone prove optimal health.

25 or more

May indicate relatively higher weight; consider muscle mass, age, and medical history.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions about the BMI calculator

Brief answers to help you understand the tool's limitations and use it more wisely.

What is the Body Mass Index (BMI)?

A calculated ratio of weight to height used as an initial indicator in adults; it does not by itself describe body composition or overall health.

Is BMI suitable for children and adolescents?

BMI for children and adolescents is not interpreted the same as for adults; it requires age, sex, and percentiles, so use the dedicated pediatric tool.

Is a BMI result a medical diagnosis?

No. The index is a screening tool and should be interpreted alongside medical history, habits, exam, and other measurements by a qualified professional.

What should I do if the result seems unexpected?

Re-enter height and weight in their correct units, then discuss the result with a professional—especially for athletes, older adults, or if there’s rapid weight change.
