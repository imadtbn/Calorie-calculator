Benefits of hydration

Benefits of drinking water regularly

Water is an essential part of the fluids the body needs to function normally. The suitable amount varies between people, but regular drinking helps support your daily needs.

◌

Supporting body functions

Fluids are involved in the work of cells, blood, saliva, sweat, and urine, helping the body maintain its natural balance.

◒

Helping regulate temperature

The body loses water through sweating, so replenishment becomes more important in hot weather or during activities that cause sweating.

↗

Supporting digestion and waste removal

Adequate fluids help digestion and waste removal via urine, sweat, and bowel movements.

✓

Low-calorie option

Replacing some sweetened drinks with water can reduce added calories and simplify your daily choices.

Simple habits

Healthy hydration tips for the day

You don't need to drink a large amount at once. Make hydration a distributed, flexible habit that fits your activity and environment.

Drink at intervalsKeep water nearby and drink with and between meals instead of waiting until you're very thirsty.
Carry a refillable bottleHaving water close makes remembering to drink easier at work, school, or while traveling.
Be more attentive in heat and exerciseYou may need more fluids in heat, during activity, or with fever, vomiting, or diarrhea.
Vary your fluid sourcesWater-rich fruits and vegetables and some unsweetened drinks contribute to total fluid intake.
Important warning: If you have heart or kidney disease, take medications that affect fluids, or are pregnant or breastfeeding, ask your doctor about the appropriate amount for you. Do not try to replace fluids by drinking large amounts quickly.
Watch your body's signals

Signs that may indicate dehydration

Dehydration can range from mild to severe. Common signs include thirst, dry mouth, headache or dizziness, fatigue, dark urine, or reduced urination.

Persistent thirstDry mouthDark urineDizziness or fatigueReduced urination
Seek urgent medical helpIf you experience confusion, fainting, difficulty breathing, rapid heartbeat, or inability to urinate, especially with vomiting, diarrhea, or heat, seek urgent medical help.
Frequently asked questions

Questions about drinking water and hydration

How many cups of water do I need daily?

There is no one-size-fits-all amount. Needs are affected by weight, activity, weather, age, and health, so the calculator provides an initial estimate, not a fixed prescription.

Is the eight-cup rule suitable for everyone?

The eight-cup reminder is common, but it's not a universal medical rule. Some people may need less or more, and fluids also come from food and other drinks.

Are coffee and tea counted toward fluids?

Many drinks contribute to total fluids, but water is a simple, low-calorie option. Watch for sugar and additives, and don't rely on sweetened beverages instead of water.

Do I need to drink more water during exercise or hot weather?

Fluid losses often increase with sweating, heat, and activity, so you may need to drink before, during, and after activity. With heavy sweating or illness, consult a professional about an appropriate rehydration solution.

Is the calculator suitable for people with heart or kidney disease?

The calculator is general and does not account for specific medical restrictions. If you have heart or kidney disease, take diuretics, or have specific fluid instructions, follow your doctor's guidance instead of the displayed number.
