Why is the method different?

BMI for children depends on growth

Bodies change with age, so a child's BMI is compared to data from children of the same age and sex. The calculator shows the approximate percentile and general classification instead of applying adult cutoffs.

How to read the classification?

According to CDC categories, less than the 5th percentile is classified as underweight; from the 5th to less than the 85th percentile is healthy weight; from 85 to less than 95 is overweight; and 95 or higher is obesity. These are screening cutoffs only and are not judgments on the child's health or appearance.

Note for parents: Do not start a restrictive diet or change the child's food based on the calculator alone. Discuss any concerns with the pediatrician or a pediatric dietitian.
Frequently asked questions

Questions about child BMI

Can adult BMI cutoffs be used for children?

No. In children and adolescents, BMI is interpreted by age and sex using percentiles, not fixed adult cutoffs.

What age range does this calculator cover?

The calculator covers children and adolescents aged 2 to 19 years, based on the CDC BMI-for-age reference tables.

Does the result classify the child's health status?

No. It is a screening tool only. Growth, weight, and height should be discussed with a pediatrician, especially with rapid changes or if the family is concerned.
