How do energy needs change during pregnancy?

The calculator uses common general values: usually no additional increase in the first trimester, about 340 calories in the second, and about 450 calories in the third. Actual needs may vary and these values may not apply to every case.

1

You often do not need a fixed extra amount; nausea, appetite, and health status matter.

2

Second trimester

General estimate of about 340 additional calories per day for singleton pregnancy.

3

Third trimester

General estimate of about 450 additional calories per day, with needs varying among women.

Food quality and follow-up matter more than the number

Calories are not the only indicator. Pregnant people need varied nutrients, adequate fluids, and nutrients like folic acid, iron, calcium, and vitamin D as recommended by their healthcare provider. Don't automatically double your food or use the number to sharply decrease or increase intake.

Important warning: If you have gestational diabetes, a twin pregnancy, severe vomiting, weight loss, or a specific dietary recommendation, do not rely on this calculator and consult your medical team.
Frequently asked questions

Questions about calories during pregnancy

Is this number my confirmed medical need during pregnancy?

No. It is a general calculated estimate for singleton pregnancy, and a doctor or dietitian should determine appropriate needs based on pre-pregnancy weight, activity, weight gain, health status, and trimester.

Do I need extra calories in the first trimester?

General references note that extra increase is often not necessary in the first trimester, but appetite, nausea, and health status vary between women.

Is the calculator suitable for twin pregnancy or gestational diabetes?

It should not be relied on for multiple pregnancy, gestational diabetes, weight loss, or severe vomiting. In these cases follow your medical team's plan.
